## 2. Recommendation

### WATCH-DEFER — revisit within 30 days.

The case for waiting is not that Accenture is a weak business. It is that the
two things that would settle the debate arrive in about three weeks, and the
price has already moved most of the way back to where it was before the debate
started.

Three facts drive this:

1. **Fiscal 2026 is already over.** Accenture's fiscal year ended August 31,
   2026 — three days before this memo's as-of date. [S1] The $13.78–$13.90
   adjusted EPS range everyone is quoting is guidance for a *completed* year
   that has not yet been reported. [S4] Calling $187.68 ÷ $13.84 a "forward"
   multiple is wrong. It is a multiple on a year already in the books. The
   genuinely forward number — FY2027 — has not been guided, and FY2027 is
   precisely the year in dispute. **[judgment]**

2. **The discount has largely closed.** ACN closed at $127.98 on June 18, 2026,
   after a 17.97% single-day decline, and touched a 52-week low of $118.15.
   [S7][S14] At $187.68 on September 2, the stock has recovered roughly 47%
   from that close and 59% from the low. **[calc]** The 8.5–9.2x trough
   multiple is gone; today's 13.6x is a normal-to-cheap multiple for a
   decelerating business, not a distressed one. **[calc][judgment]**

3. **The decisive disclosure is imminent.** Accenture released FY2025 results on
   September 25, 2025. [S11] FY2026 full-year results — Q4 bookings, FY2026
   final margin, and critically the first FY2027 revenue and EPS guidance —
   should land in roughly the same window. Waiting three weeks to see FY2027
   guidance costs very little and resolves most of the thesis. **[judgment]**

### Triggers

| Move to | If the FY2026 release shows |
|---|---|
| **INITIATE** | FY2027 LC revenue guidance ≥ 4% organic, Q4 book-to-bill ≥ 1.10, and adjusted margin guided flat-to-up — with acquisition spend guided *down* from FY2026's $9B |
| **HOLD WATCH** | FY2027 guidance 2–4% with bookings recovering but M&A spend still elevated |
| **AVOID** | FY2027 LC guidance below 2%, a second consecutive quarter of book-to-bill near 1.0, or any goodwill impairment on the FY2026 acquisition cohort |

## 3. Company and Research Date

Accenture is an Irish public limited company and global professional-services
firm spanning strategy and consulting, technology, operations, Song, and
Industry X, reporting across the Americas, EMEA, and Asia Pacific on an
August 31 fiscal year. [S1]

This memo is current through September 3, 2026. It draws on the FY2023–FY2025
Forms 10-K; the Q2 and Q3 FY2026 earnings releases and Q3 conference call; the
June 23, 2026 buyback 8-K; contemporaneous news coverage; and the September 2,
2026 market close. [S1]–[S14]

*Book-to-bill is bookings ÷ revenue, my calculation from the disclosed figures.*

The three-year record is mixed but improving on the reported lines: growth
slowed sharply in FY2024 and reaccelerated in FY2025, diluted EPS rose every
year, and GAAP operating margin recovered from FY2023 restructuring pressure —
though it did not expand in FY2025. Bookings rose 13% in FY2024, then slipped
1% in FY2025. [S1]–[S3]

FY2025 growth was broad-based: Americas revenue up 9% in local currency, EMEA
6%, Asia Pacific 4%; Managed Services grew 9% in local currency against 5% for
Consulting. Accenture generated $11.5B of operating cash flow, held $11.5B of
cash against roughly $5.1B of funded debt, and returned $8.3B via dividends and
repurchases. [S1]

The soft spot was margin quality. FY2025 gross margin fell from 32.6% to 31.9%
on higher payroll costs, and the company booked $615M of business-optimization
costs — $344M of severance and $271M of impairments, primarily on two
acquisitions that no longer fit strategic priorities. [S1]

## 5. Fiscal 2026: What the Year Actually Showed

### The quarters

| | Q2 FY26 | Q3 FY26 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $18.0B (+8% USD, +4% LC) | $18.72B (+6% USD, +3% LC) |
| New bookings | $22.1B (+6% USD) — record | $19.32B (−2% USD, −3% LC) |
| Book-to-bill **[calc]** | 1.23x | 1.03x |
| GAAP operating margin | 13.8% (+30 bps) | 17.0% (+20 bps) |
| Sources | [S13] | [S4] |

Q3 revenue of $18.72B landed slightly above the midpoint of Accenture's own
guided range of approximately $18.35B, and diluted EPS of $3.80 rose 9% and beat
consensus near $3.71. Free cash flow was $3.6B in the quarter. [S4][S7]

**The bookings number is the story, and the sequential move matters more than
the year-over-year move.** Q3 bookings of $19.32B were down 2% year over year —
but down roughly 13% from the record $22.1B set one quarter earlier, dropping
book-to-bill to 1.03x from 1.23x. [S4][S13] A book-to-bill near 1.0 means the
firm is replacing revenue rather than building backlog. That is the weakest
reading in the series above and is the metric I would watch first in the Q4
release. **[calc][judgment]**

Against that, management reported 104 year-to-date client bookings of $100M or
more, up 13%, indicating that large transformation programs held up even as
total bookings softened. [S4]

### Guidance and the guidance cut

Management narrowed FY2026 local-currency revenue growth to 3%–4% (from
3%–5%), or 4%–5% excluding an estimated one-point drag from the U.S. federal
business. It guided GAAP EPS of $13.38–$13.50, adjusted EPS of $13.78–$13.90,
and free cash flow of $10.8B–$11.5B. Q4 revenue was guided to $17.75B–$18.40B,
implying 1%–5% local-currency growth — a midpoint roughly 2.3% below
consensus. Management expected the federal headwind to anniversary and federal
to return to growth in Q4. [S4][S5][S7]

### The market reaction

The stock fell 17.97% on June 18 to close at $127.98, described by Bloomberg as
its steepest single-day decline since the company went public. [S7] Three
things drove it rather than the quarter itself: the cut to the top of the
revenue range, the Q4 midpoint shortfall, and the bookings dip arriving exactly
when investors were already primed to read weak consulting demand as AI
cannibalization. [S7] **[judgment]** Middle East revenue was hit by roughly
$400M from the Iran conflict, with management expecting further impact in Q4.
[S7]

### What has happened since

Two developments partly explain the recovery to $187.68:

- **Accelerated buyback.** On June 23, 2026, Accenture announced an additional
  $2.0B of repurchases incremental to the $300M already planned for the
  quarter, bringing expected Q4 repurchases to $2.3B. Julie Sweet stated
  explicitly that the company did not believe the share price reflected its
  position or fundamentals. [S12]
- **Continued M&A.** Accenture raised its FY2026 acquisition target spend to
  $9B — up from $3B originally and $5B at the half — and announced a $4.18B
  agreement for a majority stake in Dragos plus all of runZero and NetRise in
  OT security, alongside a steady stream of tuck-ins (Cabel, Faculty, Verum,
  Keepler, McCoy, Comware). [S7][S11]

## 6. Investment Positives

1. **Scale and client access.** Accenture serves more than 9,000 clients and
   combines industry depth, technology partnerships, proprietary assets, and
   global delivery capacity. [S1] The conclusion that this constitutes a
   durable competitive advantage smaller specialists cannot replicate is my
   inference from the disclosed scale and operating model. **[judgment]**

2. **Cash generation and capital return.** FY2025 operating cash flow was
   $11.5B; FY2026 free cash flow is guided to $10.8B–$11.5B, a $1B increase
   over FY2025 reflecting a free-cash-flow-to-net-income ratio near 1.3.
   Management committed to returning at least $9.3B to shareholders in FY2026
   and then accelerated buybacks in Q4. [S1][S4][S11][S12]

3. **AI demand is moving past pilots.** On the Q3 call, management said another
   100 clients initiated advanced-AI projects in the quarter, more clients were
   moving from pilot to production, and bookings from key emerging AI and data
   ecosystem partners were tracking to more than double versus FY2025. [S5][S11]
   Note that Accenture no longer breaks out advanced-AI bookings separately, on
   the stated rationale that AI is now pervasive — which reduces the
   verifiability of the AI narrative. [S11] **[judgment]**

4. **Vendor-neutral integration position.** Sweet has argued that Accenture can
   train and deploy people across multiple AI ecosystems and integrate new
   tools with clients' installed technology, and management treats that
   neutrality as a core advantage. This remains a management assertion, not
   independently verified. [S6]

5. **Management is buying its own stock at these levels.** The June 23
   incremental $2.0B authorization, with an explicit statement that the price
   understated fundamentals, is a costly signal rather than a talking point.
   [S12] **[judgment]**

## 7. Investment Negatives and Risks

1. **Book-to-bill has compressed to roughly 1.0.** Bookings fell 1% in FY2025,
   2% year over year in Q3 FY2026, and about 13% sequentially from the Q2
   record. [S1][S4][S13] Bookings are lumpy and do not equal contracted
   revenue, so a single quarter proves little — but a second quarter near 1.0x
   would indicate backlog is no longer building. **[calc][judgment]**

2. **Consulting demand is soft where AI substitution would show up first.**
   Q3 FY2026 Consulting grew 1% in local currency versus 5% for Managed
   Services, echoing the FY2023 10-K's disclosure of reduced client spending on
   smaller, shorter-duration consulting work. [S2][S4] Managed Services
   outgrowing Consulting is exactly the pattern the disruption thesis predicts.
   **[judgment]**

3. **Growth is increasingly bought.** FY2026 acquisition spend was raised to
   $9B against $10.8B–$11.5B of free cash flow, while the company
   simultaneously accelerated buybacks. [S11][S12] Accenture has completed 329
   acquisitions to date. Because organic and inorganic contribution are not
   cleanly separable in the disclosures, the reported 3%–4% local-currency
   growth rate cannot be read as an organic rate. This is the most important
   analytical limitation in the memo. **[judgment]**

4. **The FY2025 impairment is evidence M&A does not always work.** FY2025
   included $271M of impairments primarily tied to two acquisitions that no
   longer fit priorities. [S1] At a $9B annual acquisition run rate, the base
   for future impairment grows. **[judgment]**

5. **Federal exposure.** Roughly one point of FY2026 revenue growth pressure is
   attributed to the U.S. federal business, following procurement delays, price
   and scope reductions, and terminations described in the FY2025 filing. In
   fairness to the bull case, management expected this to anniversary and
   return to growth in Q4 FY2026 — a claim the FY2026 release will test.
   [S1][S4][S5]

6. **AI reshapes the delivery model itself.** Sweet has said AI changes the
   work, workforce, and workbench and requires upskilling plus "talent
   rotation." [S9] That supports new transformation demand but also implies
   Accenture must continuously replace obsolete skills in a business
   historically priced off billable headcount. **[judgment]**

7. **Geopolitical, currency, tax, and regulatory exposure.** The Iran conflict
   cost roughly $400M of Middle East revenue in Q3 with more expected in Q4.
   [S7] More broadly, Accenture identifies foreign exchange, OECD Pillar Two
   taxation, AI regulation, cybersecurity, and government-contracting changes
   as material uncertainties. [S1]

## 8. Management's AI Narrative and the Falsification Test

Management's central claim is that enterprises find AI easy to pilot and hard to
scale, and that this gap creates demand for Accenture's integration, data,
process-redesign, and change-management capabilities. Sweet has publicly
argued for "human in the lead" AI and framed trust and responsible-AI controls
as prerequisites for production deployment. [S5][S9][S10]

**The thesis is falsified if** AI-related announcements keep growing while
book-to-bill stays near 1.0, organic Consulting growth stays near zero, and
revenue per employee fails to rise. The decision to stop breaking out
advanced-AI bookings makes this harder to test from the outside, which itself
argues for weighting the aggregate metrics more heavily. **[judgment]**

**The thesis is validated if** book-to-bill recovers above 1.10, adjusted margin
expands, and revenue per employee rises — the last being the cleanest available
proxy for AI making Accenture's own delivery more productive rather than
merely giving it something to sell. **[judgment]**

## 9. Valuation Limitations

This memo uses a multiple and free-cash-flow-yield cross-check, not a full
valuation. Three gaps remain, and I am stating them rather than papering over
them:

- **No DCF.** No discounted cash-flow model, and therefore no explicit view on
  terminal growth — which is where the AI-disruption debate actually lives.
- **No date-matched peer set.** Directionally, Cognizant and Infosys have traded
  in the high-single to mid-teens P/E range while IBM has traded well above
  both, but I do not have same-date, same-basis multiples for IBM, Capgemini,
  Cognizant, and TCS, so I have not built a comp table. A comp table with
  mismatched dates would be worse than none. **[judgment]**
- **No organic-growth decomposition.** See Negative #3.

Given those gaps and the fact that the largest information event of the year is
weeks away, Watch-Defer is better supported than Initiate. **[judgment]**

---

## 10. Final Decision

### WATCH-DEFER.

Accenture is financially strong, generates a ~9.7% free-cash-flow yield at the
current price, and is plausibly positioned at the center of enterprise AI
adoption. But the evidence does not yet distinguish a temporarily depressed
high-quality franchise from a consulting model facing structural disruption —
and the market has already re-rated the stock 59% off its June low on the
optimistic reading of that question, before the data arrives.

The FY2026 full-year release — Q4 bookings and book-to-bill, final adjusted
margin, FY2027 guidance, and FY2027 acquisition-spend plans — is the single
most consequential missing evidence, and it is due within weeks. Deferring
until then costs a few weeks of exposure and buys the answer to the actual
question. **[judgment]**
