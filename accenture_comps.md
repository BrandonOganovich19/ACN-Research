# Accenture Comparable-Company P/E Analysis

## Focused search question

How does Accenture's mix of project-based consulting and recurring managed technology services compare with Cognizant and EPAM, and what price range does their reported-GAAP P/E imply for Accenture on October 10, 2025?

## Valuation policy

This policy was written before candidate search and has not been revised, so no recalculation was triggered by a policy change.

* **Target:** Accenture plc (`ACN`), NYSE, U.S. dollars.
* **Valuation date:** October 10, 2025. Accenture's fiscal year ended August 31, 2025, but the completed annual results were not public until the September 25, 2025 earnings release, and the audited statements I cite for EPS were not public until the Form 10-K was filed and signed on October 10, 2025. I therefore set the valuation date at the filing date so that the price date and the earnings source are both public on the same date.
* **Price convention:** unadjusted closing price in U.S. dollars for the U.S.-listed ordinary or common share on October 10, 2025. Target and peers therefore share one trading date, one currency, and a one-listed-share price basis. Dividend-adjusted or split-adjusted series are not used, because a dividend-adjusted close is a total-return figure rather than the price an investor paid that day.
* **Earnings convention:** latest full-year, reported GAAP diluted EPS publicly available by the valuation date. Quarterly, trailing-twelve-month, forecast, and adjusted EPS are not used in the calculator.
* **P/E convention:** closing share price divided by reported annual diluted EPS. A peer's P/E is the number of dollars the market pays for one dollar of that peer's annual earnings, so multiplying it by Accenture's own annual earnings per share converts the peer's pricing into a price for one Accenture share. The multiple is applied directly to Accenture's reported diluted EPS; no debt or cash bridge belongs in this equity-to-equity calculation.
* **Peer-selection policy:** mark **use** when a candidate has closely comparable consulting and managed-technology-service economics and compatible sourced price/EPS bases; mark **qualify/use** when the economics are relevant but a material scale or service-mix limitation remains; **exclude** a candidate when the business model is materially different, the target appears in its own peer set, the entry is duplicated, or same-date price and full-year positive reported diluted EPS evidence is missing. Only candidates marked use or qualify/use enter the calculator, while exclusions and reasons remain in the source table.
* **Rejection evidence written before searching.** I would reject a candidate on any of the following: the 10-K Item 1 business description shows revenue earned mainly from product licenses, hardware, or asset ownership rather than delivered professional services; the latest full-year reported diluted EPS is zero or negative; the share price is quoted in a non-USD currency or the listed instrument is an ADR representing a fraction or multiple of an ordinary share; no full-year reported EPS was public by October 10, 2025; or the only available earnings figure is adjusted, quarterly, or a forecast.

Candidate names proposed by AI partners were treated as leads, not as evidence. Every price, EPS, fiscal period, and publication date below was opened at the primary source and is cited with a section locator.

## Validation

### Hand arithmetic

I independently checked the calculator arithmetic without rounding intermediate values:

```text
CTSH P/E = $65.80 / $4.51 = 14.5898004435x
EPAM P/E = $142.38 / $7.84 = 18.1607142857x

Two-peer median = (14.5898004435 + 18.1607142857) / 2
                = 16.3752573646x

Minimum implied ACN price = 14.5898004435 x $12.15 = $177.2660753880
Median implied ACN price  = 16.3752573646 x $12.15 = $198.9593769797
Maximum implied ACN price = 18.1607142857 x $12.15 = $220.6526785714
```

The unrounded results reconcile to the calculator's displayed **$177.27–$220.65** range and **$198.96** median reference estimate.

### Prediction, then the changed-peer result

Before running the leave-one-out test I wrote down what it had to do. With exactly two peers, the median is their average, so removing either one cannot shift the estimate part-way: the surviving peer's multiple becomes the entire answer, and the range collapses to a single reference number. I predicted symmetric moves of half the spread between the two implied prices, that is (220.65 − 177.27) / 2 ≈ $21.7 in each direction, with removal of the low-multiple peer (CTSH) moving the estimate up and removal of the high-multiple peer (EPAM) moving it down.

|Changed peer set|Result|Change from two-peer median|Interpretation|
|-|-:|-:|-|
|Remove CTSH; retain EPAM only|$220.65 single-peer reference|+$21.69|The higher-multiple peer becomes the entire estimate; this is no longer a range.|
|Remove EPAM; retain CTSH only|$177.27 single-peer reference|−$21.69|The lower-multiple peer becomes the entire estimate; this is no longer a range.|

The calculator matched the prediction, which confirms the median logic is behaving as a two-element median and not silently dropping or reweighting a peer. Substantively, it confirms that peer selection is a material driver: removing either company moves the reference estimate by $21.69, or about 10.9% of the $198.96 baseline. I did not remove either peer to improve the answer; both remain in the calculator, and EPAM stays in despite being the peer whose removal would move the estimate toward the observed market price. The sensitivity does not invalidate the calculation, but it prevents treating the midpoint as a precise intrinsic value, and it is the principal reason the call remains conditional.

### Price-basis check

The policy calls for unadjusted closes, and the distinction is not cosmetic for Cognizant, which pays a dividend. Its dividend-adjusted close for the same date is $64.35 rather than $65.80. Using the adjusted series would give a CTSH multiple of 14.267849x and an implied ACN minimum of $173.35 instead of $177.27. I use $65.80 because the numerator in a P/E should be the price actually quoted for one share on the valuation date. Accenture and EPAM show no adjustment gap on that date, so the convention only binds on the Cognizant row.

### Reported-versus-adjusted robustness check

This is a check on the conclusion, not a change of policy: the calculator still runs on reported GAAP EPS. If every company were instead put on its own adjusted basis — CTSH $4.75, EPAM $10.86, ACN $12.93 — the peer multiples fall to 13.852632x and 13.110497x, and the implied Accenture range becomes roughly **$169.52–$179.11** against the same $240.94 close. The direction of the finding is therefore unchanged under either earnings definition; the premium is larger, not smaller, on the adjusted basis. The reason is EPAM: its adjusted EPS is 38.5% above its GAAP EPS, against 5.3% for Cognizant and 6.4% for Accenture. That gap is itself evidence for the qualify rather than use mark on EPAM, because a peer whose two earnings definitions diverge that widely imports more definitional noise into the multiple than the other two companies do.

### Period-mismatch check

This is the comparability limitation I take most seriously, and it does not disappear by noting it in a table. Accenture's denominator is fiscal 2025 earnings through August 31, 2025. Both peers' denominators are calendar 2024 earnings through December 31, 2024. Every multiple in this analysis therefore divides an October 10, 2025 price by earnings of a different vintage, and the peer denominators are roughly nine months staler than Accenture's.

The direction of that bias is determinable even without pulling interim figures, which the policy excludes anyway. For a fixed price, a stale denominator inflates the multiple whenever earnings have grown since the fiscal year-end: dividing by the older, smaller EPS produces a larger P/E than dividing by a current one. Both peers grew reported diluted EPS in fiscal 2024 (CTSH $4.21 to $4.51, EPAM $7.06 to $7.84), so if that direction continued the peer multiples used here are, if anything, overstated, and the implied Accenture range is biased upward.

That matters for the conclusion. The finding is that Accenture's $240.94 close sat above the peer-implied range, and an upward-biased range makes that finding harder to produce, not easier. The mismatch is a real limitation on the precision of the range, but it does not explain away the premium, and correcting it would most likely widen the gap. What the mismatch does prevent is treating $198.96 as a like-for-like fair value; the honest reading is a directional signal with a known bias against itself, not a point estimate.

### Financial reasonableness check

Accenture's own same-date P/E was **19.830453x** (`$240.94 / $12.15`), above EPAM's 18.160714x and Cognizant's 14.589800x. The market-price premium and the below-market peer-implied range are therefore internally consistent rather than a sign error: a company trading at a higher multiple than its peers must price above what its peers' multiples imply. Scale is consistent with the direction of that premium as well. On the fiscal years used here, Accenture's revenue was $69.67 billion against Cognizant's $19.74 billion (28.3% of Accenture) and EPAM's $4.73 billion (6.8% of Accenture). The remaining judgment is economic: whether Accenture's scale, service mix, growth durability, and risk justify the premium the market assigned.

## Skeptical-colleague review

The weakest-supported assumption is that Cognizant and EPAM alone establish an adequate P/E benchmark for Accenture. Cognizant is a reasonable operating comparison, but EPAM is smaller and more concentrated in digital engineering. With only two peers, the reported "median" is simply the average of two multiples and is highly sensitive to either selection.

There are also material mismatches, both between the two methods and inside the peer comparison itself:

* **Company mismatch:** `dcf.py` does not identify Accenture and contains generic inputs that do not reconcile to Accenture's financial statements.
* **Date mismatch:** the peer P/E is dated October 10, 2025, while `dcf.py` contains no valuation date.
* **Valuation-object mismatch:** the DCF values enterprise operations using FCFF, adds generic cash, subtracts generic debt, and divides by 50 million generic shares. The P/E method directly values an ACN equity share.
* **Earnings-definition mismatch:** the DCF uses FCFF; the peer method uses full-year reported GAAP diluted EPS. These can be legitimate separate methods, but they cannot be compared as two Accenture estimates until the DCF inputs are company-specific.
* **Range mismatch:** the P/E method produces a peer-implied range; the saved DCF produces one point estimate. No missing DCF range should be invented.
* **Period mismatch inside the peer test:** the target's earnings are fiscal 2025; both peers' earnings are calendar 2024. Every multiple pairs an October 2025 price with a denominator of a different vintage.

**One decision-changing question:** Can the Week 3 `dcf.py` inputs be replaced with dated, source-traceable Accenture FCFF, net cash/debt, and diluted shares while preserving the instructor-required model structure?

### Judgment after review

The skeptical criticism materially weakens confidence in using the Week 3 DCF as evidence, and it correctly identifies a period mismatch I had to quantify rather than merely disclose. It does not invalidate the correctly traced P/E calculation, and two of its stronger forms — that the result depends on the earnings definition, and that the stale denominators create the premium — fail when checked against the sources. ## Individual reflection — defending the Accenture conclusion

My company is Accenture, and my policy is to use information that was public by the valuation date, keep target and peer prices on the same trading date, use full-year reported GAAP diluted EPS for P/E, and keep reported earnings separate from adjusted earnings. Under that policy, the valuation date is October 10, 2025; Accenture's relevant earnings figure is fiscal 2025 reported diluted EPS of $12.15; and its same-date closing price is $240.94.

### Why I chose the peers

I marked **Cognizant as use** because its Item 1 business description — consulting, application development, systems integration, application maintenance, infrastructure, and business-process services delivered through an integrated global delivery model — closely resembles Accenture's labor-intensive delivery model, including the recurring managed-services work that Accenture reports under Operations. The same economics drive both: billable headcount, utilization, and wage inflation. Its fiscal 2024 reported GAAP diluted EPS of $4.51 was positive and public before the valuation date.

I marked **EPAM as qualify/use** because it earns revenue from technology consulting, digital engineering, and software development delivered through a skilled global workforce, which is genuinely comparable cost structure, but its Item 1 lists no business-process outsourcing comparable to Accenture's Operations. It is a project-engineering comparison rather than a managed-services one. Its revenue of $4.73 billion is 6.8% of Accenture's $69.67 billion, and its GAAP-to-non-GAAP EPS gap of 38.5% is far wider than Accenture's 6.4% or Cognizant's 5.3%. Its fiscal 2024 reported GAAP diluted EPS of $7.84 was positive and public before the valuation date. I admitted it with the limitation stated rather than presenting both peers as equivalent matches.

Both decisions rest on sources I opened and cited by section. No investigated candidate was excluded, and only these two qualifying companies were entered into `comps\_pe.py`.

### What the peer comparison adds

The peer method provides a market-based reference that the saved Week 3 DCF does not provide. It answers a different question from a DCF: not what Accenture's cash flows are worth in absolute terms, but what the market was actually paying for a dollar of comparable companies' annual earnings on one specific day, and what Accenture's own earnings would be worth at those prices. Using October 10, 2025 prices and reported annual diluted EPS, the peers produce P/E multiples of 14.589800x and 18.160714x. Applying those multiples to Accenture's $12.15 reported diluted EPS produces a defensible but provisional range of **$177.27–$220.65** and a median reference estimate of **$198.96**.

This comparison shows that Accenture's $240.94 closing price incorporated a premium to the selected peers, and the robustness checks show that premium is not produced by the earnings definition or by the stale peer denominators. It still does not prove that Accenture was overvalued. The premium might reflect Accenture's scale, client relationships, managed-services mix, diversification, or expected growth, and those explanations require evidence I do not yet have.
